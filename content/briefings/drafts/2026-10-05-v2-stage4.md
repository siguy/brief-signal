## The Big Picture: Compute Scarcity, Token Economics, and Stateful Agent Infrastructure

### The 2028 Compute Trap: Crusoe’s 1GW Buildout, 50% Data Center Attrition, and the Hyperscaler TCO Defense

![crusoe-1gw-compute-trap](./images/crusoe-1gw-compute-trap.jpg)

Crusoe Energy closed a $3.9B Series F at a $30.9B valuation to scale its 1GW campus in Abilene, Texas [Chase Lockmiller on 20VC (66 min watch, 14:45)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ko7nU1Img40). But behind the mega-round lies a severe physical reality: roughly 50% of announced global data center projects will fail due to grid permitting and utility interconnection queues, while electrical power distribution centers face 100-week vendor backlogs. Infrastructure providers are passing that execution risk downstream, pressuring early-stage AI startups into rigid 5-year take-or-pay contracts extending out to 2028 [Chase Lockmiller on 20VC (66 min watch, 34:20)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ko7nU1Img40).

At the same time, the price gap between hyperscalers and neoclouds is facing enterprise scrutiny. SemiAnalysis rated Google Cloud GPU clusters Gold tier in its ClusterMAX 3.0 benchmark [@SemiAnalysis_ (1 min read)](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2106038582234185737), while warning that many lower-tier neoclouds suffer from critical security isolation and orchestration flaws [Dylan on Latent Space (58 min watch)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MWX36ZYnsm0). Enterprise analyst Steve Ho explained that hyperscalers justify a 2x to 3x pricing premium over bare-metal neoclouds by bundling compliance, integrated identity and access management (IAM), analytics, and high-availability service level agreements (SLAs) [Steve Ho on The Cognitive Revolution (89 min watch, 38:15)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Sc8dSezPa4) [@kolencherry (1 min read)](https://x.com/kolencherry/status/2105000552790933584).

**Your angle with founders**

- **Concede the headline price gap:** Raw hourly GPU rates look cheaper on unbundled neoclouds. If a founder only needs throwaway batch compute with zero compliance overhead, they should take the spot discount.
- **Deconstruct the hidden infrastructure bill:** Ask what it costs in engineering salary to hand-roll SOC2 compliance, IAM boundaries, VPC peering, and Slurm node-failover recovery on top of bare metal. Unbundled hosting shifts operational risk straight to their balance sheet.
- **Challenge the 2028 commitment:** "Are you signing a rigid 5-year take-or-pay contract on unbuilt data center capacity that has a 50% failure rate, or can your contract scale dynamically as model efficiency shifts?"
- **Where GCP wins:** Gold-tier GPU and TPU infrastructure with Flexible Committed Use Discounts (CUDs) and serverless Cloud Run scaling—giving founders enterprise compliance and guaranteed capacity without multi-year balance-sheet traps.

### The 80% Margin Blueprint: Higgsfield’s $1B Run-Rate, Anthropic’s $518B Deficit, and the Open-Weight Routing Engine

![higgsfield-80-percent-margin](./images/higgsfield-80-percent-margin.jpg)

Generative video platform Higgsfield scaled to a $1B revenue run-rate in 18 months, with CEO Alex Mashrabov revealing the unit economics powering the jump: hosting and fine-tuning open-weight models delivers gross margins exceeding 80%, compared to just 20% to 30% when proxying calls to closed frontier APIs [Alex Mashrabov on 20VC (76 min watch, 34:10)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jszn8rFtxm4). By dynamically routing over 40% of production traffic to open models fine-tuned on customer interaction data, Higgsfield turned generative inference into an 80%+ gross margin SaaS business.

This margin blueprint lands as leaked S-1 filings reveal the financial reality facing frontier labs: Anthropic logged an $8B operating loss on $4.66B revenue in 2025 and committed to a staggering $518B in future compute obligations [Harry Stebbings on 20VC (81 min watch, 04:15)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNH5YtuK5UE) [Scott Galloway on Pivot (58 min watch)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zsOTuGaa1hk). Meanwhile, the Ramp AI Index showed corporate AI spend fell 5.2% as price wars between OpenAI and Anthropic compress token costs [@arakharazian (1 min read)](https://x.com/arakharazian/status/2105740752886309163). As high-volume builders shift routine tasks to open models like DeepSeek and GLM on OpenRouter [@nicbstme (1 min read)](https://x.com/nicbstme/status/2105464782811697542), application winners are escaping the single-lab margin squeeze [@dharosaurus (1 min read)](https://x.com/dharosaurus/status/2105453525849518421).

**Your angle with founders**

- **What they'll say:** "Frontier API price cuts make it unnecessary to host our own models." Grant that API prices are tumbling, but point out that frontier labs are running half-trillion-dollar compute deficits to subsidize those prices.
- **The reframe that actually holds:** Passing every prompt through closed frontier endpoints caps your gross margins at 20–30%. The sustainable SaaS winners treat models like compilers: route routine tasks to fine-tuned open weights you own, and call frontier reasoning only when ambiguous judgment demands it.
- **The question to leave behind:** "What percentage of your production inference runs through proprietary endpoints where price increases or rate limits immediately crush your gross margin?"
- **Where GCP wins:** Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (FKA Vertex AI) Model Garden lets teams evaluate, fine-tune, and route between open Gemma and frontier Claude or Gemini under one API—protecting margins and eliminating single-provider dependency.

### The Battle for Agent State: OpenAI DevDay (Dots, Space, Decisions API) vs. Enterprise Context Gravity

![openai-devday-agent-state](./images/openai-devday-agent-state.jpg)

OpenAI expanded from a stateless API provider into a stateful enterprise platform at DevDay 2026 [OpenAI DevDay Recap (1 min read)](https://openai.com/index/devday-2026-recap). The lab introduced "Dots"—proactive, always-on background agents provisioned with dedicated cloud Linux virtual machines to execute code and browser tasks [Ari Weinstein & Nikunj Handa on Latent Space (41 min watch, 04:40)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9OkBD2-MDU). Alongside Dots, OpenAI debuted "Space," an agent-native collaboration suite featuring native documents, spreadsheets, and slides directly inside ChatGPT to eliminate the latency of browser-based agent automation in external tools [@danshipper (1 min read)](https://x.com/danshipper/status/2104982021626007885), plus the Decisions API for sub-cent routing [@kunchenguid (2 min read)](https://x.com/kunchenguid/status/2104998134279762141).

This launch accelerates a fundamental architectural battle over where enterprise memory and context live [@JayaGup10 (8 min read)](https://x.com/JayaGup10/status/2043505841127760066). Frontier labs want to host the execution runtime, persistent episodic memory, and organizational documents behind proprietary APIs, effectively capturing enterprise state [@yashhsm (10 min read)](https://x.com/yashhsm/status/2105380670688346335). But for enterprise buyers, locking institutional knowledge and agent memory inside a single model vendor's vault recreates legacy SaaS lock-in with higher stakes. Durable moats are forming in the customer-governed data layers that manage agent state across models.

## Seller's Edge: The State Gravity Moat (Why Intelligence Commoditizes but State Compounds)

In enterprise AI, raw model intelligence is deflating while organizational state compounds. Edition #17 taught selling the substrate beneath commoditizing models, and Edition #32 diagnosed the pass-through margin trap; this week reveals where durable platform lock-in actually forms. Model prices drop 50%+ annually, and reasoning capabilities are continually matched across frontier labs and open weights. But state—episodic agent memory, execution history, permission graphs, and business context—accumulates over time and creates real switching costs.

Look at OpenAI DevDay launching Space and Dots to capture workspace files and persistent agent VMs behind proprietary endpoints [@danshipper (1 min read)](https://x.com/danshipper/status/2104982021626007885), contrasted with Anthropic's $518B compute obligations [Harry Stebbings on 20VC (81 min watch, 04:15)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNH5YtuK5UE). Model vendors are racing to capture state because they know stateless API token revenue suffers from relentless price erosion [@arakharazian (1 min read)](https://x.com/arakharazian/status/2105740752886309163) [@JayaGup10 (8 min read)](https://x.com/JayaGup10/status/2043505841127760066). When an enterprise lets its agent memory and operational context accumulate inside a closed proprietary API, switching models stops being a procurement choice and becomes an institutional crisis.

In your next meeting, stop debating benchmark point differences between models. Ask: *"Where does your agent's accumulated memory and organizational state live—inside a model provider's proprietary vault, or inside your own governed cloud storage where any model can query it zero-copy?"* Help the founder architect a decoupled stack: persistent state anchored in their own VPC, treating foundation models as interchangeable, stateless reasoning engines.

Propose sizing current workloads on dynamic pools rather than signing speculative 5-year bare-metal leases.

The Agent Platform enables asymmetric routing: run fine-tuned open Gemma on TPUs or Gemini 3.8 Flash for routine classification, reserving Claude for complex reasoning. Whiteboard the founder's prompt mix to route >40% of calls to open weights.

Google Cloud keeps agent state, memory, and governance in customer-controlled storage using BigQuery Cross-Cloud Lakehouse, Spanner queues, and AlloyDB AI. Challenge founders to build agent memory in their own VPC so switching models requires zero state migration.
